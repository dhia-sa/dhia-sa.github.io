E-commerce Analytics with dbt + DuckDB

An end-to-end analytics engineering project. About 100k raw e-commerce orders are modelled with dbt into a tested star schema and business-ready marts covering sales, delivery, customer RFM, cohort retention and sellers. The results are served in a Streamlit dashboard, and the whole build runs on a laptop in about 10 seconds with no cloud warehouse.

The problem

Raw marketplace exports are messy. Customers get a new ID with every order, reviews are duplicated, categories are in Portuguese, and payment totals don't always match order values. Before anyone can answer "how are sales trending, why are customers unhappy, and do they come back?", the data needs a clear, tested model with agreed definitions. That's what analytics engineering is for.

Key insights (Jan 2017 – Aug 2018)

GMV (product value, excluding freight) across about 98k valid orders, with an average order value of about R$ 137.

2.3★ vs 4.3★

Average review for late vs on-time orders. About 7% of delivered orders are late, with the highest rates in CE, BA and RJ. Delivery is the biggest driver of satisfaction.

Data quality decisions

  • Real customers, not order IDs: customers are keyed on customer_unique_id, because the source issues a new customer_id for every order. Without this, retention would be wildly wrong.
  • One review per order: the most recently answered review is kept.
  • Load as text, cast in staging: this preserves leading zeros in zip codes and makes every type conversion explicit.
  • Category fallback: untranslated or missing categories fall back to the Portuguese name or unknown, so no rows disappear.
  • Warn, don't fail: payment vs order-value mismatches are flagged as warnings, because they are real source quirks, not pipeline bugs.

Quality & CI

66 data tests cover keys, relationships, accepted values, ranges and singular business rules. On every push, GitHub Actions lints the SQL with sqlfluff and compiles all models. When Kaggle credentials are available, it also runs the full build and publishes the dbt docs (with lineage) as an artifact.

What I learned

Most of the work in analytics engineering is deciding what the data means: which ID is the real customer, which review counts, when a mismatch is a bug and when it's a quirk. Encoding those decisions as tested, documented dbt models turns one-off analysis into something a team can trust and build on.
